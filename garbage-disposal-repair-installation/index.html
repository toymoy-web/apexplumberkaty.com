Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Katy, TX

A garbage disposal that hums but won't spin, leaks from underneath, or has gone completely silent is one of the most common kitchen calls we get in Katy. Most of the time it's a jam or a worn seal, not a sign the whole unit needs replacing, and a plumber can tell you which one you're dealing with in the same visit.

Why This Happens in Katy

Newer Katy kitchens, especially in communities like Cane Island, often come with a disposal pre-installed that eventually needs its first real service, while older homes near the historic district may be adding one for the first time under a sink that's never had the wiring or plumbing for it. Either situation is routine work for a plumber already familiar with both housing eras.

Signs to Watch For

Signs You Need Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation

Humming but Not Spinning

Usually a jam. The motor is running but the flywheel can't turn.

Leaking From Underneath

Often a cracked housing or worn seal, not something a plumber can seal back up.

Unusual Grinding Noise

Can mean something's lodged that shouldn't be, like a utensil or bone fragment.

No Power at All

Check the reset button on the unit first, then the breaker, before assuming it's dead.

Bad Odor Even After Cleaning

A disposal that still smells after regular cleaning often has food trapped somewhere the blades can't reach on their own.

Diagnosis

How We Diagnose It

A plumber checks the reset button and breaker first, since that resolves a real share of "dead" disposals on its own. From there it's a check for a jam using a disposal wrench through the bottom of the unit, a test of the motor and wiring, and an inspection of the housing itself for cracks, which is the difference between a repair and a straightforward replacement. If the unit is original to the home, the plumber also checks whether the electrical connection meets current code before reinstalling anything.

Options

Reset/Repair or Replace?

Repair Usually Makes Sense When

  • The unit hums but doesn't spin (a jam)
  • No leaking from the housing
  • The unit is under 6 years old

Replacement Usually Makes Sense When

  • The housing itself is leaking
  • The motor has burned out
  • The unit is undersized for the household and struggles with normal daily use

Before We Arrive

What to Check or Shut Off

Never put your hand inside the disposal, even when it's off. Use the reset button on the bottom of the unit or the breaker instead.

If it's leaking, shut off the water to the sink and unplug or breaker the unit before we arrive.

Don't pour standing water or grease down it while it's jammed. That can make the eventual repair messier.

Note whether it hums, is silent, or trips the breaker. That one detail narrows the diagnosis a lot before we even arrive.

FAQs

Common Questions

Why does my disposal hum but not spin?

That's almost always a jam. A plumber can usually clear it in the same visit using a disposal wrench, without replacing the unit.

Can you install a disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, including any additional wiring or plumbing the installation needs.

Is a leaking disposal repairable?

Rarely. A cracked housing usually means replacement rather than a repair, since a crack in the metal casing itself isn't something that can be sealed back up reliably or safely over the long term.

What size disposal do I need?

It depends on household size and how the kitchen is used day to day. A plumber can recommend the right horsepower on the visit rather than guessing from the size of the old unit alone.
