Whole-Home Repiping in Katy, TX

Had More Than One Leak in the Last Year or Two?

A single leak is usually just a single leak. A second or third leak in different parts of the same home within a short window is a different signal entirely, one that often means the underlying pipe material itself is failing broadly, not that you've had unusually bad luck.

Local Factor

Why This Happens in Katy

Homes in Katy's older neighborhoods built with galvanized steel supply lines are the most common repiping candidates, since that material corrodes from the inside in a way that eventually affects the whole system, not just one section. Copper systems can also reach a point where repiping makes more sense than continuing to chase individual leaks.

Signs to Watch For

Signs You Need Repiping & Whole-Home Pipe Replacement

Multiple Leaks in a Short Time Span

The clearest sign the underlying pipe material is failing broadly.

Discolored Water Throughout the Home

Often a sign of internal corrosion in galvanized supply lines.

Consistently Low Water Pressure

Can indicate significant internal corrosion restricting flow throughout the system.

A Home With Original Galvanized Pipe

A real, recognized candidate for repiping regardless of current symptoms.

Diagnosis

How Repiping Works

A plumber maps the home's existing plumbing system and plans a route for new PEX or copper lines, working room by room to minimize disruption. Old pipe is removed or capped, new lines are run and pressure-tested, and fixtures are reconnected before any drywall or access points get closed back up.

Options

Full Repipe or Continue Repairing Individually?

Continued Individual Repairs Fit When

  • This is the first leak the home has had
  • The pipe material is otherwise in good condition
  • The home is relatively new construction

A Full Repipe Fits When

  • You've had multiple leaks in different areas
  • The home has original galvanized supply lines
  • Water pressure and discoloration issues are systemic

Before We Arrive

What to Check or Shut Off

Keep a record of every leak your home has had, including date and location; it helps confirm whether repiping is genuinely warranted.

Ask whether your specific symptoms (discoloration, pressure) point to a systemic issue before committing to a full repipe.

Get a written scope before starting, since repiping is a significant project.

Don't wait until a third or fourth leak to consider the bigger picture; the pattern is usually clear by the second.

FAQs

Common Questions

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

Typically a few days for an average-sized home, depending on accessibility and how much of the home needs new lines.

Do I need to move out during a repipe?

Not usually, though water will be off during working hours. Most homeowners stay in the home during the project.

Is PEX or copper better for a repipe?

Both are valid options with different tradeoffs; a plumber can walk through which fits your home and budget better.
