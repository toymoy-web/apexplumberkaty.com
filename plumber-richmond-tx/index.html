Neighborhoods

Historic Downtown and the New-Construction Ring

Downtown Richmond, near Morton Street and the Fort Bend County Courthouse, is genuinely historic, with homes and commercial buildings dating back generations. Ring that historic core with newer master-planned communities like Aliana, Harvest Green, and Pecan Grove, spread across the 77406, 77407, and 77469 ZIP codes, and you get two very different plumbing profiles inside the same city limits.

Housing Era

Cast Iron Downtown, PEX in the New Builds

Older homes near downtown Richmond are more likely to still be running cast iron or galvanized drain lines, which corrode from the inside in a way that's invisible until a clog turns into a full backup. Newer builds in Aliana and Harvest Green run PEX and modern PVC, so the actual failure points there tend to be fixtures and installation, not the pipe material itself.

What Richmond Calls About Most

Two Different Repair Profiles

Downtown, the most common call is a stubborn drain or sewer clog tied to aging cast iron. In the newer developments, it's more often a fixture issue or a water heater questions for a home that's still under a year or two old. A plumber who already knows which part of Richmond a call is coming from can bring the right expectations, not just the right tools.

Local History

A City That's Been Around Since 1837

Richmond was incorporated in 1837, making it one of the older municipalities in this part of Texas, and its historic district still reflects that: the Fort Bend County Courthouse on Jackson Street dates to 1908, and the Long-Smith Cottage at the Fort Bend Museum is even older. A plumbing system's age tends to track a neighborhood's age fairly closely, and downtown Richmond's is old enough that original infrastructure is a real, expected factor in almost every call there. Any repair or replacement still has to meet the current Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) as adopted in Texas, whatever the age of the pipe it's replacing.

Storm Season

Fort Bend County's Flood History

Fort Bend County, including Richmond, has a documented history of serious flood events tied to the Brazos River watershed. That history is part of why sump pump reliability and backflow prevention get taken seriously here, whether the home is a century-old property downtown or a new build in Harvest Green.

Before We Arrive

If You Have a Burst Pipe or Active Leak in Richmond

Richmond's mix of century-old cast iron near downtown and modern PVC out in Aliana and Harvest Green means a burst pipe looks different depending on where in the city it happens, but the same first steps apply either way.

Shut Off the Main Valve

Stop the water at its source before anything else, if you know where your shutoff is located.

Kill Power to Wet Areas

Cut electricity at the breaker to any area with standing water before anyone walks through it.

Clear the Immediate Area

Move people, pets, and valuables away from the active leak or flooding.
