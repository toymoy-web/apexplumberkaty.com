What to Shut Off During a Plumbing Emergency (and What to Document for Insurance)

By the Katy Apex Plumbing team · September 21, 2026

The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency matter more than almost anything that happens after. Knowing exactly what to do, and in what order, limits the damage before help arrives, and it's easier to think clearly about this now than in the middle of an actual emergency.

Shut Off Water First, at the Right Point

If the problem is isolated to one fixture, a supply line under a sink or a toilet, look for that fixture's own shutoff valve first, usually a small valve on the wall or floor right where the supply line connects. Shutting off there stops the specific leak without cutting water to the whole house.

If the leak's source isn't obvious, or if it's clearly a main line rather than a single fixture, go straight to the home's main water shutoff, typically near where the water line enters the house or at the meter outside. Knowing where this is before an emergency happens is the single most useful thing a homeowner can do in advance.

Cut Power Before Cleanup Starts

Standing water and electricity are a dangerous combination. If water has reached an outlet, appliance, or any electrical fixture, shut off power to that area at the breaker before anyone walks through it, even to shut off water. If there's any doubt about whether it's safe, treat it as unsafe until it's confirmed otherwise.

This matters even for water that looks shallow. It doesn't take much to create a real electrical hazard, and this is not a step to skip in the rush to stop the leak.

What to Document Before Anything Gets Cleaned Up

Once the immediate danger is handled, take photos and video of the damage before starting any cleanup, the water's extent, any affected belongings, and the apparent source if it's visible. Insurance claims move faster and more smoothly with this documentation than with a description after the fact.

Keep any receipts related to emergency mitigation, a wet-vac rental, temporary repairs, since many policies cover reasonable steps taken to limit further damage. Contact your insurer promptly rather than waiting until repairs are already underway, since some policies have specific reporting windows.

Common Mistakes People Make in the Panic

The most common mistake is trying to locate the main shutoff for the first time during the emergency itself, wasting critical minutes while water continues flowing. The second most common is turning off power at an individual outlet rather than the breaker, which doesn't actually cut power to that circuit and provides false confidence in an area that's still live.

A third mistake is starting cleanup before documenting anything, out of an understandable urge to just get the water up and stop the damage. A quick round of photos, even mid-panic, takes under a minute and can matter a great deal to how smoothly an insurance claim goes later.

Building Your Own Emergency Reference Now

The best time to find your main water shutoff, your electrical panel, and your insurance policy's claims contact information is today, not during an active emergency. Take a photo of each, the shutoff valve's location, the breaker panel with circuits labeled, and your policy's emergency contact number, and keep them somewhere accessible on your phone.

This five-minute exercise, done once, removes the single biggest source of wasted time during an actual emergency: not knowing where anything is when it matters most.

What Your Insurance Actually Expects From You

Most homeowners policies include a requirement to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage once a problem is discovered, which is part of why documenting first and then acting to stop the immediate source, rather than the reverse, aligns with what insurers actually expect. Letting a known leak continue for an extended period without action can affect how a claim is evaluated, separate from the original cause of the damage itself.

Understanding this ahead of time removes the guesswork during an actual emergency: shut off the source, document what you can safely and quickly document, and then contact both a plumber and your insurer, rather than trying to figure out the right order while water is actively causing damage.

A Simple Family Plan Worth Having

If more than one person lives in the house, make sure everyone, not just whoever normally handles home maintenance, knows where the main shutoff and breaker panel are located. An emergency doesn't wait for the most familiar person to be home, and a household where only one person knows these basics is one bad-timing coincidence away from a much worse outcome.

This is a five-minute conversation worth having once, ideally while walking through the actual locations together rather than just describing them, so everyone has real, hands-on familiarity rather than a vague idea of where things are.

Common Questions

Should I try to stop a leak myself before calling a plumber?

Yes, shutting off the water source is safe and recommended. Leave the actual repair itself to a licensed plumber.

What if I can't find the main shutoff during an emergency?

Call our emergency line immediately. A plumber can talk you through locating it over the phone or dispatch quickly if needed.

Do I need to keep damaged items for the insurance adjuster?

In many cases yes, at least until the adjuster has seen or been shown documentation. Check with your insurer directly.

Is emergency plumbing service more expensive than scheduled service?

Often yes, given the after-hours dispatch involved, though the exact cost is always disclosed upfront before any work actually begins on your home.
