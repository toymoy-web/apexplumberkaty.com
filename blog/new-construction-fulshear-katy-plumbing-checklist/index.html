New Construction in Fulshear-Adjacent Katy: What to Check Before You Move In

By the Katy Apex Plumbing team · September 21, 2026

A builder walkthrough covers the basics, does the water run, do the drains clear. It doesn't always catch the installation issues that show up a few months in, after the warranty clock has already started, which is exactly the window a more thorough check can still catch.

What the Standard Walkthrough Tends to Miss

A builder walkthrough is usually quick and focused on obvious function, not on whether a connection was properly sealed or a fixture was installed to a standard that'll hold up over years, not just the first few weeks. Slow leaks at a supply line connection, a water heater sized a little small for the household, or a P-trap that wasn't seated correctly are all things that pass a basic walkthrough and show up later.

In a fast-growing area like Fulshear, where entire sections of a community like Cross Creek Ranch or Jordan Ranch are built out in a compressed timeline, installation quality can vary more between houses than in a slower, more established build.

What's Worth Checking in the First Month

Run every fixture, including ones that don't get daily use like a guest bathroom or a utility sink, and check underneath for any dampness after running water for a few minutes. Check the water heater's actual capacity against the household's real usage rather than assuming the builder sized it correctly, since an undersized unit is a common and often overlooked issue in new construction.

Test water pressure at multiple fixtures at once, running a shower and a faucet simultaneously, to catch any restriction that a single-fixture test would miss. And locate the main water shutoff before you need it, since new homeowners often don't know where it is until an emergency forces the question.

Knowing What's a Warranty Issue vs. Normal Service

Builder warranties typically cover installation defects for a defined period, but they don't cover routine wear, and the window for filing a warranty claim closes faster than most people expect. An independent inspection early on, separate from the builder's own walkthrough, can catch a genuine installation issue while it's still covered rather than after the warranty has lapsed.

This is worth doing even if everything seems fine. New construction hides issues well in the first few months, and catching one on the builder's dime beats paying for it later as a normal repair.

Questions to Ask Your Builder Directly

Before closing, ask specifically what the water heater's rated capacity is and whether it was sized based on the home's actual bedroom and bathroom count, not a generic default. Ask what warranty period covers plumbing specifically, since overall structural warranties and plumbing/mechanical warranties often run on different timelines. Ask whether a pre-drywall plumbing inspection was performed and whether documentation exists, since that's a meaningfully more thorough check than a post-completion walkthrough can ever be.

Builders in a fast-growing area like Fulshear generally have clear answers to these questions since they're building the same models repeatedly. A vague or evasive answer to any of them is itself useful information about how thoroughly that particular build was inspected during construction.

What a Second Opinion Actually Costs vs. Saves

An independent plumbing inspection during the warranty period typically costs far less than a single uncovered repair after the warranty lapses. For a new build in a growing Fulshear community, where entire phases go up on compressed timelines, that inspection is cheap insurance against exactly the kind of installation issue that a rushed schedule can introduce.

It's a small expense relative to the price of the home itself, and it's one of the few plumbing costs that's genuinely optional today but potentially expensive to skip later.

What Happens If You Wait Too Long to Report an Issue

Builder warranties don't just expire on a calendar date, they can also be affected by how quickly an issue is reported once discovered. Waiting months to mention a suspected problem, even within the warranty window, can complicate a claim if the builder argues the delay contributed to additional damage. Reporting promptly, even for something that seems minor, protects your position regardless of how the builder ultimately responds.

This is a real, practical reason to schedule that independent inspection early rather than waiting to see if something develops into an obvious problem on its own, since catching an issue while it's still small and reporting it promptly is meaningfully better than discovering it after months of undetected progression.

What Growing Communities Like Cross Creek Ranch Do Well

To be fair to the builders working in Fulshear's fastest-growing communities, the scale and pace of construction has also meant a lot of repetition and refined process, the same floor plans and systems built repeatedly tend to have fewer novel mistakes than a one-off custom build would. The issues that do show up are usually specific installation oversights on an individual home, not systemic design flaws repeated across every house in a phase.

That's a genuinely useful thing to know: a specific issue on your home isn't necessarily a sign the whole community has problems, it's usually isolated and fixable once identified, which is exactly why an independent check early on is worth the modest cost. Approaching a new build with this balanced view, neither assuming everything is perfect nor assuming the worst, sets realistic expectations for what a new home in a fast-growing area actually needs during its first year or two.

Common Questions

How long is a typical builder plumbing warranty in Texas?

It varies by builder, often one year for workmanship, so check your specific purchase agreement for the exact terms and any exclusions.

Should I hire my own inspector or use the builder's?

An independent inspector, not affiliated with the builder, gives an unbiased second opinion worth the modest cost.

What if I find an issue after the warranty expires?

It becomes a normal repair, which is exactly why catching issues during the warranty window matters so much for a newer home in a growing area.

Are plumbing warranties transferable if I sell the home?

Some are, within the original coverage window. Check the specific builder's warranty documentation for transfer terms, since this varies meaningfully between builders and even between phases of the same community.
